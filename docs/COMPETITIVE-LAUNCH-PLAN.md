# Content Protect competitive launch plan

**Status:** working plan — 16 November 2026. This is an operational assessment, not legal advice, a provider contract, or authority to activate a paid service. Every external claim below is either a public competitor claim or a future acceptance criterion; it is not a claim that Content Protect presently delivers it.

## Decision summary

Content Protect has a stronger-than-usual pre-launch control base for an early product: encrypted storage, rights declarations, evidence capture, operator step-up authentication, dispute controls, Stripe/Yoti test safeguards, recovery verification and a passing automated test suite. It is **not commercially ready** because its real discovery provider, live age verification, live billing, legally approved notice delivery, retention execution, independent audit custody and launch governance remain deliberately fail-closed.

The correct immediate target is therefore an invitation-only, **free, non-commercial and non-explicit-image** pilot. It must validate the human operating model before any claim of automated discovery or takedown is made.

## Market reference, used carefully

BranditScan publicly markets hourly automated scanning, AI/facial recognition, automated DMCA notices and Google delisting, social-media removals, removal timelines/screenshots, privacy and doxxing tooling, concierge support, and agency/studio plans. It also markets Premium at USD 69/month and White Glove at USD 149/month (public pages checked 16 November 2026).

These are **vendor marketing claims, not independently audited performance facts**. They are still a useful customer-expectation baseline: a mature buyer will expect fast discovery, credible evidence, reliable routes for notice delivery, transparent outcomes, human support and predictable billing.

## Exact gaps to close

1. **Discovery:** commercial TinEye agreement/key, DPA/transfer assessment, approved media scope, caps/no-auto-top-up, and measured production acceptance tests.
2. **Takedown desk:** specialist review of notice template and jurisdiction routes, named accountable operator, approved delivery routes, outcome taxonomy and measured follow-up process.
3. **Pilot governance:** creator agreement, explicit scope/consent, adult/rights confirmation, exclusion criteria, withdrawal/deletion path, safety escalation contacts and weekly review record.
4. **Identity:** either live Yoti approval plus receipt test, or a documented legal/commercial decision that a free pilot uses a different approved verification route.
5. **Billing:** Stripe live account/prices, live Checkout + webhook + cancellation + refund test, pricing decision and customer communications. It is not required for a genuinely free pilot.
6. **Launch controls:** signed DPIA, provider processor register/transfer evidence, tabletop exercise, penetration test, retention execution approval, separate audit-export custody and signed launch manifest.
7. **Commercial proof:** pilot conversion, cost per reviewed match, false-positive rate, removal/delisting outcomes by route, response times, retention and cancellation experience.

## Roadmap and gates

### Phase A — controlled free pilot with Luxandra

**Target duration:** 2–4 weeks after the minimum privacy/verification decision is approved.

**Objective:** validate a human-reviewed case workflow with one invited creator, with no charge, no automatic notices, no promise of discovery coverage and initially no intimate reference media.

**Deliverables**

- Signed pilot invitation/terms, authority declaration and informed consent record.
- Confirmed adult/identity route and named escalation contacts.
- One historical outcome recorded only with creator authority; it must distinguish `reported`, `acknowledged`, `removed at original URL`, `deindexed`, and `not verified`.
- Five consented non-explicit acceptance tests after the discovery source is approved, or a documented no-provider dry run if it is not.
- Weekly pilot dashboard: scan attempts, leads, review verdicts, spend, response time, withdrawals, disputes and incidents.

**Dependencies:** owner approval; privacy/counsel decision; either approved TinEye route or a provider-free controlled dry run; adult/authority evidence.

**Risks:** processing special-category media without approved basis; treating a single removal as coverage proof; unauthorised agency instructions.

**Cost:** GBP 0 platform billing if provider-free. If TinEye is enabled, use only a pre-approved smallest package with a hard monthly cap and no auto top-up. Legal/verification pricing is quote-dependent.

**Owner:** Director (commercial decision), privacy/copyright counsel (lawfulness and wording), operator (evidence/review).

**Exit condition:** five documented acceptance tests, zero unresolved high-severity safety/privacy incidents, written go/no-go review and no unsupported marketing claim.

### Phase B — discovery and takedown validation

**Target duration:** 4–8 weeks after Phase A exit.

**Objective:** validate one approved image source and one legally approved notice route with a cohort of up to five consented creators.

**Deliverables**

- TinEye commercial activation evidence, DPA/transfer assessment and query cap.
- Counsel-approved copyright notice template and route register.
- At least 20 reviewed leads with source, verdict and evidence; do not count duplicates as separate discovery wins.
- Separate measurements for `notice prepared`, `creator approved`, `delivered`, `acknowledged`, `source removed`, and `Google delisted`.
- A weekly cost/quality report and a manual incident/dispute drill.

**Dependencies:** Phase A exit; TinEye commercial approval; counsel review; named operator rota.

**Risks:** false positives, invalid claimant authority, provider policy breach, unbounded query spend.

**Cost:** estimated GBP 100–500/month for a tightly capped pilot **excluding** legal advice and supplier quotes; treat this as a planning range only until a TinEye quote is received. One-off legal review/penetration test are quote-dependent and should be approved separately.

**Owner:** Director, operator lead, counsel and provider account owner.

**Exit condition:** four weeks of evidence, documented false-positive rate, route-level outcome metrics, no unresolved high-severity incident and written decision to proceed/not proceed.

### Phase C — commercial activation

**Target duration:** 6–10 weeks after Phase B exit.

**Objective:** allow a small paid cohort only after every customer-impacting control is evidenced.

**Deliverables**

- Live Yoti or approved alternate verification route; consented end-to-end receipt test.
- Stripe live Checkout, webhook, cancellation and refund acceptance evidence; approved plans, refund/cooling-off copy and no unexpected auto-renewal configuration.
- Signed DPIA, privacy notice, terms, processor register, transfers, retention approval, incident tabletop and independent penetration-test remediation decision.
- Separate audit-export destination and current signed launch-governance manifest.
- Published service boundary: reviewed matching and approved notices, not a guarantee of removal or global coverage.

**Dependencies:** all launch-gate controls, provider contracts, formal pricing and director approval.

**Risks:** legal liability, payment disputes, underpriced human operations, unproven support capacity.

**Cost:** indicative fixed operating budget GBP 500–2,000/month before labour, plus payment fees. Stripe UK standard-card processing is currently published as 1.5% + 20p per successful domestic transaction; use live Stripe pricing at activation. Supplier, verification and legal fees require written quotes.

**Owner:** Director, privacy/copyright counsel, finance, security reviewer and operations lead.

**Exit condition:** `/api/health/ready` returns every required operational gate true and `productionReady: true`; a written launch decision and rollback owner exist; no gate uses placeholder evidence.

### Phase D — competitive scaling

**Target duration:** 6–12 months of measured operation after commercial activation.

**Objective:** earn, rather than market, a credible position against mature providers.

**Deliverables**

- Multiple approved discovery sources and source-level coverage/quality measurements.
- Trained operations desk, standard follow-up cadence and platform/host route library.
- Cohort reporting, agency workflows, controlled whitelist/identity tools and transparent proof of outcomes.
- Product decisions backed by unit economics: cost per query, review, delivered notice, confirmed removal and retained customer.

**Dependencies:** enough lawful volume; provider relationships; measured quality; funded human operations.

**Risks:** scaling unreviewed automation, inaccurate takedowns, unprofitable service levels, reputation damage.

**Cost:** do not promise a fixed amount. Budget should be built from actual pilot unit economics plus legal/support labour, provider quotes and reserve for disputes. Treat all paid expansions as a new approval.

**Owner:** Director and operations/product leads.

**Exit condition:** at least 100 real reviewed cases (or counsel-approved equivalent test set), route-level evidence, stable false-positive/quality metrics, audited operating controls and an independently supportable commercial claim.

## Immediate no-cost work order

1. Use the outcome taxonomy above in the operator process; do not turn a reported link into a claimed removal without rechecking the exact URL.
2. Prepare the five acceptance-test cases with non-explicit, owned test material; do not transmit them to a commercial provider until that provider gate passes.
3. Prepare a one-page pilot consent/authority pack for counsel review; it must state the exact provider, retention, transfer and withdrawal route before signing.
4. Build a weekly pilot evidence report with the metrics named above.
5. Keep Stripe live, video scanning, automatic notice delivery and public commercial claims off.

## Timing estimate

- **First safe controlled pilot:** 2–4 weeks after minimum external privacy/verification decisions.
- **First paying customer:** 3–5 months, conditional on provider/counsel approvals and successful pilot evidence.
- **Stable commercial service:** 5–8 months, conditional on live controls, measured operations and support capacity.
- **Credible competitive operational level:** 12–24 months, because this requires historical outcome data, source/platform relationships and trained human operations—not just software.
