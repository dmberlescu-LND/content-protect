# Urgent impersonation and personal-data removal playbook

Status: operational draft. This is a safety and evidence workflow, not legal advice. Obtain qualified advice before making a legal claim, sending a formal notice or escalating a disputed case.

## Scope

Use this playbook where a creator or another affected person reports an urgent public listing that appears to:

- impersonate them or falsely associate them with sexual, escort or other services;
- use their photograph, name, stage name, telephone number or messaging account without permission;
- expose intimate or otherwise sensitive personal information; or
- create a credible safety, stalking, harassment or reputational risk.

This route is separate from a copyright claim. Do not state that the reporter owns copyright unless that authority has been recorded and reviewed. A listing may justify an impersonation or personal-data removal request even where copyright ownership is unclear.

## First 15 minutes: protect and preserve

1. Create a restricted case reference in the approved operational system. Do not put names, telephone numbers, private media, credentials, identity documents or full URLs in source control, chat logs or governance manifests.
2. Capture the exact public URL, the date and time in UTC, the visible claim, the contact detail used and a minimal page capture. Preserve the source and checksum. Avoid downloading or redistributing intimate images.
3. Record who is affected, whether they are the person depicted, whether they have authority to act, and the immediate harm reported. This record is restricted evidence, not a public product note.
4. Do not contact the uploader, call the advertised number, make a public accusation or promise a removal time.
5. If there is an immediate threat, extortion, stalking, credible risk of physical harm or a child-safety concern, instruct the affected person to use the relevant emergency or law-enforcement channel. Content Protect is not an emergency service.

## First hour: use the right route

1. Start with the host or platform's official abuse, privacy, impersonation or content-removal channel. A host request is the route most likely to remove the source page.
2. Use a copyright/DMCA channel only if the affected person has confirmed ownership or authority for the identified work. Keep that basis specific to the exact image or other work.
3. Where a telephone number, name or other personal data is published without authority, request removal of the entire listing and the contact detail, describe the impersonation and ask the platform to preserve uploader/account records. Do not request personal information about the uploader unless counsel or law enforcement requires it.
4. Keep search de-indexing separate from source removal. Only start a search-engine request after confirming that the relevant result is indexed and that the policy route fits the facts.
5. Record the submitted text, recipient/channel, confirmation/reference, timestamp and requested remedy in the case timeline. Never mark a listing as removed merely because the request was sent.

## Minimum evidence and approval gates

Before sending any notice, an authorised operator must confirm:

- the precise URL and a current page capture;
- the claimant's or authorised agent's authority for the specific request;
- the legal/policy basis actually used (impersonation, personal data, non-consensual imagery, copyright, or another platform policy);
- the exact notice text, recipient and disclosures; and
- the affected person's approval where the notice discloses their identity, contact details or personal-data claim.

For high-risk, cross-border, disputed, repeat or intimate-image cases, stop automated follow-up and escalate to the named safety/legal owner.

## Follow-up and closure

1. Check the source URL and any reported search result at documented intervals. Preserve a new capture for a verified outcome.
2. Use status labels precisely: `reported`, `acknowledged`, `source removed`, `de-indexed`, `disputed`, or `unresolved`. Do not treat one as another.
3. If the host does not respond, review the current terms and official escalation routes before a registrar, host/CDN or search escalation. Do not send duplicate notices.
4. Close only after recording the verified outcome, remaining risk, creator communication and any deletion/retention action. Keep disputed cases frozen until reviewed.

## Product boundary before launch

The current Content Protect application has evidence capture, human-review and approval controls for copyright-style cases. It does not yet support automated delivery of impersonation or personal-data notices. Those cases must remain operator-led and legally reviewed until a separate approved template, claimant declaration and delivery route are implemented and tested.
