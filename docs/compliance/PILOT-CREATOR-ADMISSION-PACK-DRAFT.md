# Content Protect — pilot creator admission pack (draft)

**Status:** draft for UK privacy/copyright counsel review — 16 November 2026. Do not send, sign, collect identity documents, upload media, or use this as a substitute for the Privacy Notice, Terms, DPIA, age-verification process or a provider agreement.

This pack is intended for an invitation-only, free, non-commercial first pilot. It must be completed per creator, stored outside Git, and assigned a restricted evidence reference only after counsel approves the wording and processing basis.

## 1. Pilot invitation summary

Content Protect is testing a limited, human-reviewed workflow for protecting creator-owned media. The pilot is not a promise of internet-wide discovery, automatic removal, legal representation, Google delisting, a specific response time or a specific outcome.

No external notice will be delivered without a separate case-specific creator approval. The first cohort uses non-explicit reference images only. Video scanning, automatic removal and paid billing are excluded.

## 3. Creator declarations — counsel must approve exact wording

The creator must actively confirm each separate declaration; no pre-ticked boxes, bundled consents or oral assumption is sufficient.

1. **Authority.** “I confirm that I own the submitted reference material or am authorised by the rights holder to ask Content Protect to process it for the stated pilot purpose.”
2. **Accuracy.** “I will identify authorised uses and provide accurate information. I understand a visual match is a lead for review, not proof of infringement.”
3. **Scope.** “I understand this is a limited, free pilot. Content Protect does not guarantee discovery, removal, deindexing, response times or legal representation.”
4. **Separate media consent.** “I explicitly consent to Content Protect processing each reference file I choose to submit for private matching and case evidence, subject to the approved Privacy Notice. I understand the content may reveal sensitive information and I may withdraw this consent by deleting eligible reference media, subject to legal-hold and retention obligations explained to me.”
5. **Provider disclosure.** “Before any external matching provider is used, I will be shown the provider identity, country/transfer position, purpose, retention information, query limit and cost limit that apply to my file. I will not submit media to that provider until this is displayed and separately approved in the product.”
6. **Case-specific notice approval.** “I understand that a prepared notice must show the recipient, jurisdiction/route, claimant identity/capacity, target URL and exact final text. I must approve that exact notice before Content Protect can request delivery.”
7. **No credentials.** “I will not provide social-media, creator-platform, email, payment or third-party account passwords to Content Protect.”

## 4. Explicit exclusions for the first pilot

- No minors or content involving minors.
- No intimate/explicit reference images, videos, biometric profiling or facial-recognition feature.
- No upload before an approved identity/age and rights route is complete.
- No TinEye or other provider transmission until commercial permission, transfer/DPA review and a non-explicit acceptance test are approved.
- No live notice delivery, no automatic takedown and no paid subscription while `CONTENT_PROTECT_PILOT_MODE=free` is active.
- No assertion that an unverified public page is unlawful, that a platform will act, or that an outcome will be achieved.

## 5. Consent withdrawal, deletion and safety

The creator must receive an accessible route to withdraw consent, request deletion and report urgent safety issues. Explain before admission that:

- withdrawal stops future optional processing but does not retroactively make previously lawful processing unlawful;
- a legal hold, open dispute, statutory accounting record or required retained audit evidence can limit immediate deletion;
- urgent harm, impersonation, personal-data exposure or credible threats are handled through the restricted safety process, not by reusing the copyright notice workflow;
- the creator can receive a plain-language explanation of the status and applicable retention path.

## 6. Pilot evidence and outcome taxonomy

For every pilot item, record only the minimum necessary evidence and use one status at a time:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `reported` | Creator or authorised representative supplied a URL; no outcome implied. |
| `acknowledged` | Recipient/platform confirmed receipt; no removal implied. |
| `removed-original-url-verified` | The exact previously captured URL no longer resolves to the reported material on a dated recheck. |
| `deindexed-verified` | A defined search-result URL was removed from the named search surface on a dated recheck; source removal is separate. |
| `not-verified` | Evidence is unavailable, ambiguous or stale. |
| `disputed-or-frozen` | Do not continue the case without counsel/operator review. |

Record the recheck date, original URL, evidence reference and reviewer. Do not store screenshots, personal contact details or media in this document.

## Why this must remain a draft

The ICO states that special-category processing requires an Article 6 lawful basis and a separate Article 9 condition, and that explicit consent must be specific, affirmative and withdrawable. High-risk processing requires a DPIA before it begins. Counsel must apply those requirements to the actual product, provider contract, transfer position and pilot scope; this draft does not decide them.
